# Model card — NAARI glucose-excursion model (`gb-0.1-synthetic`)
**Task.** Among moments where CGM glucose ≤ 180 mg/dL, estimate whether glucose exceeds 180 mg/dL at any point in the next 120 minutes. 180 mg/dL is the consensus "time above range" threshold, chosen for this model only; it is not a PCOS diagnostic criterion.
**Inputs.** Previous 2 h (24 × 5-min samples) of glucose, heart rate, steps; hour of day; latest verified HbA1c. Inference is refused unless ≥ 90 % of samples exist in all three streams and a verified HbA1c is on file.
**Models.** Baseline: logistic regression on current glucose only. Model: gradient boosting (150 trees, depth 3). Both fit on training patients only; split grouped by patient (30 train / 10 test).
**Data.** SYNTHETIC — `naari/synth.py` (seed 7): 40 fictional patients, 14 days, 5-min sampling. No real patient or public dataset was used to train this delivered model. Public/de-identified datasets are being considered separately; see `REAL_DATA_STRATEGY.md`.
**Held-out results (synthetic test patients, n = 6,022 windows, prevalence 0.164).** See `models/metrics.json`. Gradient boosting: AUROC 0.978, AUPRC 0.889, recall 0.736, precision 0.858, Brier 0.046. Baseline: AUROC 0.461.
**Critical limitation.** The generator ties HbA1c and meal-response size to one hidden variable, so the model largely recovers how the data was built. These numbers say nothing about clinical performance. Outputs are uncalibrated scores, not validated risks; no uncertainty interval is produced.
**Not intended for** diagnosis, treatment decisions, or use on real patients. **Next step:** evaluate a separately trained research model on an appropriately licensed real CGM dataset with documented provenance and a participant-grouped split. The current trained artifact remains synthetic-only until such a study is completed and independently evaluated.
